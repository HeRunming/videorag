### 4.3 `F09` — reject

## 6. Why the lecture cases cannot support broad ambient claims

The four canonical sources are lecture recordings. Across the inspected
Oxford, MIT, and Yale material:

1. the semantic content is carried by lecturer speech, official transcript or
   captions, chalkboard/OCR, or static diagrams;
2. visible changes are usually writing, pointing, slide changes, or talking
   heads, not source-world non-text events whose evolution answers a question;
3. non-speech audio is generic room/classroom/chalk/microphone sound and does
   not encode the substantive lecture answer; and
4. machine AST labels are unstable, speech-dominated, generic, or implausible
   for the source context.

These sources can validate board/OCR, transcript, static-diagram, duplicate
route, and speech-outage packet mechanics. They cannot establish broad
ambient-audio, event-centric, or dynamic-visual validity.
