## 1. First principle

The project is not blocked on another model run. It is blocked on the right
scientific input.

The highest-value next action is to create a small number of defensible,
human-adjudicated evidence families under the existing fail-closed rules. Do
not compensate for missing construct validity with more infrastructure,
larger models, more development-source authoring, or broader claims.

## 2. Preserve before changing anything

Before new work, preserve these invariants:

1. **R21 remains a failed historical authoring result.**
2. **R22 remains permanently closed and NO-GO.** Never rerun it, reuse its
   receipt, redraw a reserve as R22, or install replay output as success.
3. **R23-A remains implementation/replay only.**
4. **R23-B v9 remains design-ready only** until independent authorization,
   identity proof, sealed fresh inputs, and all runtime/review requirements
   exist.
5. **R23-C remains exposed development calibration.** It can never count as
   fresh or untouched scientific evidence.
6. **Untouched identities remain private.** Construction, distribution, and
   unlock remain prohibited until their separate gates pass.
7. **Machine-provisional families remain non-human and non-scientific.**
8. **No scientific episode runs before Gate A, Gate B, Gate C, and the causal
   interlock authorize it.**

Treat these as research-integrity constraints, not administrative preferences.

## 4. Critical path: Gate A repair

Gate A is the current bottleneck. A valid repair requires all of the following,
not just a complete candidate table.

### 4.1 Source authority

For each proposed pilot source group, close:

- exact source and production lineage;
- access and acquisition authority;
- processing and annotation authority;
- privacy and sensitive-content review;
- derivative and release boundaries; and
- future-test eligibility.

Do not infer rights from public visibility, dataset reputation, or a working
download route.

### 4.2 Semantic source prescreen

Complete the frozen source-uniform human prescreen for all proposed sources.
The goal is not to confirm machine hypotheses. It is to decide whether the
complete source can support defensible authoring without shortcutting,
recurrence, source-world ambiguity, or modality substitution.

Audio-bearing proposals require the designated listening workflow. Machine
audio labels are routing aids, not semantic findings.

### 4.3 Fresh authoring

For every surviving slot, create fresh human-authored:

- question and answer;
- atomic required and forbidden claims;
- evidence intervals and modalities;
- candidate support derivations;
- alternative-route inventory;
- intervention hypothesis; and
- reason the task is not answerable through a simpler shortcut.

Development-source machine templates must not be promoted unchanged.

### 4.4 Fill the missing structural profile

The current prospective matrix is not sufficient. Repair must honestly address
the missing cross-modal and far/multi-hop coverage, including the currently
unfilled vision-plus-speech and tri-modal targets. Do not relabel a weak
near/local item to satisfy a quota.

If the source pool cannot produce the required profile under unchanged
criteria, replace sources or issue NO-GO. Do not weaken the construct.

### 4.5 Human workflow readiness

Before distribution:

- complete reviewer training and calibration;
- freeze role assignments and source blindness;
- bind all packets and exposure ledgers;
- verify author/verifier and saturation-auditor separation;
- define adjudication and reopen rules; and
- independently re-audit Gate A.

Only an explicit Gate A GO may authorize the 12-family construct pilot.

## 5. Gate B: construct-validation mini-pilot

After Gate A GO, run the human workflow in the frozen order:

1. independent author/verifier review;
2. blind full-source alternative-evidence search;
3. claim/evidence validation;
4. leave-one-out minimality tests;
5. modality-drop and substitution checks;
6. shortcut, recurrence, transcript/OCR, and derived-leak closure;
7. intervention isolation and restoration-route validation;
8. adjudication; and
9. new-reviewer saturation audit with reopen-on-discovery.

### Required outputs

- accepted or rejected question/answer/claim records;
- all accepted SESs found under the protocol;
- necessity-group or necessary-channel determination;
- disagreement and rejection reasons;
- pair-isolation and restoration evidence;
- role-complete exposure and labor ledgers;
- H1/H2 decisions; and
- a Gate B GO/REPAIR/NO-GO decision.

### Stop rule

If acceptable H1/H2 yield cannot be obtained without changing definitions,
relaxing saturation, hiding alternative routes, or reusing touched evaluation
sources, stop or redesign. A low yield is a scientific result.

## 6. In parallel: Gate C baseline repair

Human construct validation and baseline competence can proceed in parallel,
but scientific episode execution requires both.

### 6.1 Do not rerun consumed holdouts

Existing fictional competence and holdout results are binding for their exact
versions. Improvements require:

- a new versioned adapter or prompt;
- development-only debugging;
- a newly frozen unseen suite; and
- a new independent one-shot evaluation.

### 6.2 Minimum competent system matrix

The paper needs at least:

- a credible direct audiovisual row;
- a single-shot learned RAG row;
- a learned ReAct row;
- a learned evidence-sufficiency row;
- deterministic protocol controls; and
- oracle/partial-evidence diagnostics that separate retrieval from later
  failure stages.

Every learned row must use the observed-evidence lock, common budgets,
structured invalid accounting, exact model/prompt/runtime identity, and no
silent deterministic substitution.

### 6.3 Gate C evidence

Gate C should require, on source-free unseen competence cases:

- reliable structured actions;
- accurate evidence-state diagnosis;
- valid claim/citation finalization;
- recovery after exogenous restoration;
- failure handling without private information;
- reproducible runtime and model identity; and
- independent audit.

Acquisition-ready, adapter-ready, or load-ready is not competence-ready.

## 7. Causal execution interlock

Before any H2 model episode:

- freeze exact CLEAN/BLOCKED/RESTORED/NO_TRIGGER state surfaces;
- verify that RESTORED and NO_TRIGGER share identical prefixes;
- bind the restoration trigger to policy-blind execution state;
- freeze sessions, caches, context truncation, and tool budgets;
- preassign intention-to-treat arms and counterbalancing;
- pass visibility diffs and metadata-only leakage classifiers;
- freeze invalid-run handling and replay rules; and
- verify route certificates and common supported-answer endpoints.

Any state artifact that reveals the arm, target evidence, answer, or trigger
invalidates the causal interpretation.

## 8. First scientific run: low-N H2 diagnostic

Only after Gate B GO, Gate C GO, and causal-interlock pass:

1. freeze the accepted H2 mini-pilot;
2. freeze systems, prompts, models, budgets, and estimands;
3. execute the complete assigned C/B/R/N matrix;
4. independently replay trajectories and metrics;
5. report every invalid and negative row;
6. separate retrieval failure from grounding, stopping, and recovery;
7. aggregate within source before uncertainty analysis; and
8. issue Gate D GO/REPAIR/NO-GO.

### What would justify scale-up

Scale only if the mini diagnostic shows at least one robust paper-relevant
pattern across multiple sources, such as:

- similar CLEAN accuracy but materially different blocked-state calibration;
- acquired evidence without supported claims;
- support/citation divergence not explained by retrieval recall;
- successful diagnosis but failed recovery;
- restoration effects absent in NO_TRIGGER; or
- distinct competent-controller behavior under a shared backend.

Do not scale merely because the pipeline runs.

## 9. CVPR scale path

### Preferred target

- at least **60 H2 families**;
- at least **30 source groups**;
- at least **12 untouched test groups**;
- source-disjoint development, validation, and test;
- unchanged construct and saturation criteria; and
- frozen learned systems and analysis before test unlock.

### Restricted fallback

A smaller 60-H2/20-source/8-test configuration may support a descriptive
diagnostic paper only. It must not support controller-superiority or broad
generalization claims.

### Scale in tranches

1. authorize a first bounded review tranche;
2. audit H2 yield, labor, disagreement, rights attrition, and source diversity;
3. continue only if the unchanged-criteria path remains feasible;
4. freeze the final corpus;
5. unlock untouched construction only under the ledger's governance;
6. freeze the main evaluation;
7. execute once; and
8. run final construct, leakage, replay, rights, and sentence-level claim
   audits.

## 12. Common failure modes to avoid

1. Treating a READY packet/tool/index as scientific GO.
2. Counting machine hypotheses as human findings.
3. Calling modality routing “modality necessity.”
4. Calling a selected clip “necessary” without alternative-SES closure.
5. Reusing touched development sources as untouched test.
6. Rerunning or semantically rescuing R21/R22.
7. Running stronger models before competence and construct gates.
8. Reporting only answer accuracy or retrieval recall.
9. Hiding invalid runs or replacing them with deterministic outputs.
10. Scaling annotation before measuring H2 yield and labor.
11. Publishing restricted identities, paths, receipts, or reserve details.
12. Writing the paper claim first and weakening gates to fit it.
